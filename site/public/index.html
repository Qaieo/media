October 13, 2026 explainer

What is Perceval?

Perceval is Quandela's open-source Python framework for photonic quantum computing. If Qiskit is the SDK for superconducting qubits, Perceval is the equivalent for photons.

Photonic quantum computers use particles of light as qubits. They run at room temperature, network naturally over fiber, and are the bet of companies like Quandela, PsiQuantum, Xanadu, and ORCA. The programming model is different from gate-based machines: you design linear optical circuits (beam splitters, phase shifters, detectors) and the framework simulates them or targets real hardware.

What Perceval gives you:

  • Circuit design for linear optical quantum computing, with a simulator backend for development.
  • Hardware access to Quandela's photonic QPUs through the cloud.
  • Error mitigation (as of v1.3.0) for dealing with the probabilistic reality of photonic hardware: loss, detector imperfections, partial distinguishability.
  • A growing algorithm library for the problems photonics is naturally good at: boson sampling, graph problems, quantum machine learning.

It's Apache-licensed, actively developed (monthly-ish releases), and currently the most alive open-source project in photonic quantum software. Which brings us to the bigger story.

October 13, 2026 analysiseditorial

The last photonic framework standing

Something quiet happened in photonic quantum software: Xanadu archived Strawberry Fields, its flagship photonic quantum computing library (854 stars, the reference implementation for continuous-variable photonic circuits since 2018). Then it archived MrMustard, the successor library. Both now read-only. No new features, ever.

That leaves Perceval as the only actively developed open-source framework for programming photonic quantum computers. One framework, one company (Quandela) behind it, for an entire hardware paradigm.

Why this matters more than it looks. Open-source frameworks are where a field's knowledge compounds. Qiskit's dominance didn't just help IBM; it trained a generation of quantum programmers who default to IBM's stack. Every archived framework is a small brain drain: tutorials rot, Stack Overflow answers age out, new students find a graveyard where the onboarding used to be.

The risk is a single point of failure. If Quandela pivots, gets acquired, or just slows down, photonic quantum software has no bench. PsiQuantum, the best-funded photonic player, has no open framework at all. The field's software story becomes "one French company's SDK," which is a fragile foundation for an entire approach to quantum computing.

The opportunity is concentration. Scarcity focuses attention. Every photonic quantum developer now has exactly one serious open-source home, which means Perceval's community, docs, and ecosystem get all the oxygen. If you're learning photonic quantum computing in 2026, there is one answer to "where do I start," and this site exists to make that answer as good as it can be.

That's the thesis here: track Perceval closely, cover the field honestly, and help the last framework standing become the foundation photonic quantum deserves.

October 13, 2026 analysiseditorialhardware

Photonic quantum hardware landscape, October 2026

Photonic quantum computing had its breakout year. Private capital into photonic hardware hit $2.1 billion in 2025, about 21% of all quantum tech investment, overtaking superconducting as the largest hardware subcategory (per Future Markets' 2026 report). Here's the field as it stands.

Three architectures, three bets. Continuous-variable systems, led by Xanadu, encode information in squeezed light and target quantum machine learning through PennyLane. Discrete-variable systems (PsiQuantum, Quandela, ORCA, QuiX) work with individual photons in linear optical circuits, aiming at fault-tolerant universal machines. Hybrid spin-photon (Photonic Inc, backed by Microsoft) links silicon spin qubits with telecom photons for distributed architectures.

PsiQuantum is the capital leader: a $1B Series E in September 2025 (BlackRock, Temasek, Baillie Gifford; $7B valuation), a $100M CHIPS Act agreement finalized in September 2026, and the Omega silicon-photonic chipset fabbed at GlobalFoundries on a standard 300mm process. Brisbane and Chicago utility-scale sites are under construction, with leadership shifting to ex-AMD/Xilinx chief Victor Peng as interim CEO for the scaling phase. The bet: photons don't generate heat or feel electromagnetic interference, so chips link over standard fiber and the million-qubit roadmap is a manufacturing problem, not a physics problem.

Xanadu became the first pure-play photonic quantum company on a major public market, listing on NASDAQ and the TSX as XNDU in March 2026 via SPAC (~$302M gross proceeds, $275M PIPE). The hardware story in 2026 is packaging: an average 0.085 dB/facet edge-coupling loss, meaning 98%+ of the optical signal survives each fiber-to-chip interface. In photonics, loss is the enemy that compounds at every interface, so this is a genuine milestone, not marketing, though edge coupling is one of roughly 16-17 loss contributors, and analysts estimate another 5-10x system-wide reduction is still needed for fault tolerance. The software story is the opposite: Strawberry Fields and MrMustard are archived, and PennyLane carries the stack alone.

Quandela is the deployment leader in Europe: the 12-qubit Belenos system serves 1,200+ researchers across 30 countries via cloud, and its EuroHPC sibling Lucy was delivered to CEA's TGCC and inaugurated in April 2026. Perceval, its open framework, is the subject this publication tracks most closely.

ORCA Computing is the operational-simplicity play: its PT Series system was installed at the UK's National Quantum Computing Centre and powered up and operational within 36 hours (June 2025). Rack-mounted, room-temperature (cryogenics confined to the detectors), accessible via NVIDIA's CUDA-Q. When a quantum computer installs faster than enterprise SaaS, the deployment model has changed.

Don't forget Hefei. USTC's Jiuzhang 4.0 manipulated and detected 3,050 photons in Gaussian boson sampling (reported in *Nature*, May 2026), still the academic high-water mark. China's photonic program runs quieter than the venture-backed Western companies but keeps setting the experimental bar.

What to watch. Whether PsiQuantum's manufacturing bet converts capital into deployed systems. Whether Xanadu's public-market discipline accelerates or starves its hardware roadmap. Whether Quandela's European deployments compound into a developer ecosystem around Perceval. And whether anyone else open-sources a serious photonic framework, or the field consolidates around one.

September 18, 2026 releasev1.3.0error-mitigation

Perceval v1.3.0 'Sir Galaad': error mitigation and a new computation workflow

The biggest Perceval release in a while. v1.3.0 "Sir Galaad" ships a whole new error mitigation feature, significant enough that it required rewriting the high-level classes (everything above Processor/RemoteProcessor).

Error mitigation, built in. The new classes slot into a Computer for automatic use and mitigate the probabilistic results you get running on real photonic hardware. Available algorithms: detector loss balancing, HOM/g2 mitigation, loss mitigation, and compilation averaging. Usable as individual classes or through the ErrorMitigationFactory.

A new computation workflow. SimulatedComputer and RemoteComputer replace Processor and RemoteProcessor (minus the Experiment), so they can be reused easily, with noise linked to the Computers. The old path still works but should not be used in new code; the docs include a migration table.

Why it matters: error mitigation is the difference between a photonic QPU demo and a photonic QPU result you can defend. Baking it into the framework's main path, rather than leaving it as user-land code, is a maturation signal for the whole stack.
