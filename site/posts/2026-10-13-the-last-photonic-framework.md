Something quiet happened in photonic quantum software: **Xanadu archived Strawberry Fields**, its flagship photonic quantum computing library (854 stars, the reference implementation for continuous-variable photonic circuits since 2018). Then it archived **MrMustard**, the successor library. Both now read-only. No new features, ever.

That leaves **Perceval** as the only actively developed open-source framework for programming photonic quantum computers. One framework, one company (Quandela) behind it, for an entire hardware paradigm.

**Why this matters more than it looks.** Open-source frameworks are where a field's knowledge compounds. Qiskit's dominance didn't just help IBM; it trained a generation of quantum programmers who default to IBM's stack. Every archived framework is a small brain drain: tutorials rot, Stack Overflow answers age out, new students find a graveyard where the onboarding used to be.

**The risk is a single point of failure.** If Quandela pivots, gets acquired, or just slows down, photonic quantum software has no bench. PsiQuantum, the best-funded photonic player, has no open framework at all. The field's software story becomes "one French company's SDK," which is a fragile foundation for an entire approach to quantum computing.

**The opportunity is concentration.** Scarcity focuses attention. Every photonic quantum developer now has exactly one serious open-source home, which means Perceval's community, docs, and ecosystem get all the oxygen. If you're learning photonic quantum computing in 2026, there is one answer to "where do I start," and this site exists to make that answer as good as it can be.

That's the thesis here: track Perceval closely, cover the field honestly, and help the last framework standing become the foundation photonic quantum deserves.
