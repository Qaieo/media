Photonic quantum computing had its breakout year. Private capital into photonic hardware hit **$2.1 billion in 2025**, about 21% of all quantum tech investment, overtaking superconducting as the largest hardware subcategory. Here's the field as it stands.

**Three architectures, three bets.** Continuous-variable systems, led by **Xanadu**, encode information in squeezed light and target quantum machine learning through PennyLane. Discrete-variable systems (**PsiQuantum**, **Quandela**, **ORCA**, QuiX) work with individual photons in linear optical circuits, aiming at fault-tolerant universal machines. Hybrid spin-photon (**Photonic Inc**, backed by Microsoft) links silicon spin qubits with telecom photons for distributed architectures.

**PsiQuantum** is the capital leader: $1B Series E in September 2025 (BlackRock, Temasek, Baillie Gifford), a $100M CHIPS Act agreement, and the Omega silicon-photonic chipset fabbed at GlobalFoundries on a standard 300mm process. Brisbane and Chicago utility-scale sites are under construction, with leadership shifting to ex-AMD/Xilinx chief Victor Peng as interim CEO for the scaling phase. The bet: photons don't generate heat or feel electromagnetic interference, so chips link over standard fiber and the million-qubit roadmap is a manufacturing problem, not a physics problem.

**Xanadu** became the first pure-play photonic quantum company on a major public market, listing on NASDAQ as XNDU in March 2026 via SPAC (~$302M gross). The hardware story in 2026 is packaging: 0.085 dB/facet edge-coupling loss, meaning 98%+ of the optical signal survives each fiber-to-chip interface. In photonics, loss is the enemy that compounds at every interface, so this is a genuine milestone, not marketing. The software story is the opposite: Strawberry Fields and MrMustard are archived, and PennyLane carries the stack alone.

**Quandela** is the deployment leader in Europe: the Belenos system serves 1,200+ researchers across 30 countries via cloud and has been delivered to EuroHPC infrastructure at CEA. Perceval, its open framework, is the subject this publication tracks most closely.

**ORCA Computing** is the operational-simplicity play: its PT-2 went from contract signing to installed at the UK's National Quantum Computing Centre in 36 hours. Rack-mounted, room-temperature (cryogenics confined to the detectors), accessible via NVIDIA's CUDA-Q. When a quantum computer installs faster than enterprise SaaS, the deployment model has changed.

**Don't forget Hefei.** USTC's Jiuzhang 4.0 detected 3,050 photons in boson sampling (August 2025), still the academic high-water mark. China's photonic program runs quieter than the venture-backed Western companies but keeps setting the experimental bar.

**What to watch.** Whether PsiQuantum's manufacturing bet converts capital into deployed systems. Whether Xanadu's public-market discipline accelerates or starves its hardware roadmap. Whether Quandela's European deployments compound into a developer ecosystem around Perceval. And whether anyone else open-sources a serious photonic framework, or the field consolidates around one.
